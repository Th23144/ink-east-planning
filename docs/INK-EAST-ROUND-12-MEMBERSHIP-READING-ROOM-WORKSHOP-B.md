## 0. What this workshop is deciding / 这一步究竟决定什么

The question is no longer:

> “Which content should be locked behind Membership（会员）?”

The correct question is:

> **“When all normal published content remains public, what additional relationship, recurring utility, scarce participation, experience, service courtesy or capability value is genuinely worth paying for?”**

Workshop B must distinguish:

- value that should simply be free platform functionality;
- value that is legitimately scarce/costly and may support Membership;
- value that could create recurring member utility;
- value that would accidentally buy authority, distribution or editorial favoritism and must be rejected;
- value that sounds attractive but is too operationally expensive or premature.

---

# 1. Four possible Membership value theses / 四种会员价值主张方向

These are **product choices**, not rules.

## Option A — Supporter-first（支持者优先）

Membership is primarily:

- support Ink & East（墨与东方）;
- receive a supporter relationship/status;
- receive editorial updates / selected participation opportunities;
- receive light courtesy benefits.

### Strength
Simple, aligned with open content, low product complexity.

### Risk
If the emotional relationship is not strong enough, recurring paid conversion/retention may be weak.

---

## Option B — Utility-first（工具价值优先）

Membership is primarily:

- deeper Reading Room workspace（阅读室工作空间）;
- advanced reading organization;
- private annotation/collections/export/digests;
- other recurring personal reading utilities.

### Strength
Clear recurring functional value.

### Risk
Easy to create artificial deprivation if basic reading/account functionality is deliberately weakened for free users.

---

## Option C — Participation-first（参与关系优先）

Membership is primarily:

- greater access to limited-capacity editorial/community experiences;
- salons / office hours / feedback panels / Ask capacity;
- closer relationship with the publication.

### Strength
Difficult to copy; creates identity and community belonging.

### Risk
Human editorial labor becomes the bottleneck; payment must not turn into governance/editorial favoritism.

---

## Option D — Hybrid supporter + utility + scarce experience（支持关系 + 工具 + 稀缺体验混合）

Membership uses:

- Supporter Relationship（支持者关系） as the emotional core;
- recurring Reading Room utility as day-to-day value;
- limited-capacity editorial/community participation as scarce value;
- bounded service courtesy/priority;
- selected anti-abuse capability acceleration where justified.

### Assistant provisional recommendation / 助手暂定建议

**Option D（方案 D）** is the strongest current direction to test.

Reason:

- content remains open;
- supporter-only value may be too abstract alone;
- utility-only design can become a disguised SaaS paywall;
- participation-only design is labor-heavy;
- a hybrid gives Membership several independent reasons to remain valuable without selling knowledge access.

This is **NOT a user decision**.

---

# 2. Evaluation standard for every benefit / 每一项权益的评估标准

Each candidate should pass these questions:

1. Does it create real additional value, or merely remove an artificial free-user limitation?
2. Is the value recurring enough to support ongoing Membership?
3. Does it consume real scarce resources / operational cost?
4. Can it scale without damaging editorial quality?
5. Does it accidentally buy epistemic authority, governance power, Recognition or organic ranking?
6. Does it fragment the unified Community System（统一社区系统）?
7. Does it create an unfair publishing/editorial fast lane?
8. Is it understandable to users without complex tier charts?
9. Does it remain useful if a member never uses Custom Reading（定制解读）?
10. Can the benefit remain evolvable/versioned rather than permanently hard-coded?
