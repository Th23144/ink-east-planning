A book, written for one.

The Custom Ebook Studio is the workshop side of Ink & East. One editor takes one commission and writes one book — eighteen to fifty thousand words, eight to twelve weeks, three projects a quarter at most. It is private work. It does not appear in the magazine.

§ 貳 The premise

For people who have one book in them, and one only.

Most of the people who write to us are not professional writers. They are someone with a long and particular life, or a family whose history is about to be lost, or the founder of a small house that has finally earned the right to put its origin in print. They have the material for one book. They do not have the next ten years to learn how to shape it.

A memoir, written in the first person, with the editor sitting in the margins. A brand book or company book — not advertising, but the record of why a place came to exist. A private essay collection, kept for circulation among friends, family, or a small readership only the author knows. Sometimes a single long letter to a child. Once or twice a year, a piece of fiction that needed an outside hand.

The work is slow and the calendar is short. We take three of these commissions a quarter. Beyond that the editor cannot give the attention the form requires, and we would rather decline than thin the writing.

§ 肆 What you keep — and what we don't promise

The book, the rights, the things we will not say.

Copyright (BRIEF §17.4)

Two licences are available. By default — the Standard Licence — the client receives personal use of the final manuscript and any commercial use named in the scope letter; Ink & East retains its baseline authorial rights, its editorial method and structural experience, and the right to display the project anonymously as a case (no name, no excerpt of your private material). For projects that should leave no trace at all, the Full Rights Buyout is quoted separately on top of the project fee; once paid, Ink & East does not display, reuse, or refer to the project content.

Source material (BRIEF §17.5)

Anything you give us — text, photographs, archive, interview audio, family stories — must be yours to give. The client warrants that the material is held under lawful use. Ink & East does not carry liability for third-party rights in client-supplied source.

Revisions (BRIEF §17.6)

Every project includes two reasonable rounds of revision after the manuscript is delivered. Work beyond that scope is re-quoted by the hour or by stage; we'd rather name a new figure than quietly absorb the time.

Delivery format (BRIEF §17.7)

PDF and DOCX are the default deliverables. An EPUB-ready file is available as an add-on, named in the scope letter. Print formatting, ISBN registration, distribution, platform listing, and marketing are not part of the project unless quoted as separate work.

What we will not promise (BRIEF §17.8)

We do not promise publication. We do not promise sales. We do not promise an Amazon ranking, an offer from a publisher, a return on investment, or any commercial outcome. The studio is paid to make a well-written private book; the world afterwards is not in the contract.

Briefs we don't take

Medical, legal, financial, and academic ghostwriting fall outside the studio's brief and are declined on receipt. We also turn down work we cannot place an editor on, and we reserve the quiet right to decline any brief without a long explanation.

Pricing · placeholder copy

A custom ebook begins at a meaningful starting price — closer to a small new car than to a paperback — and is given as a fixed quote, in writing, before any work begins.

No public price list, no tiered packages. Each project is quoted in step 叄 of the nine, after the editor has read the brief. You always see the full figure, and the payment split, before any deposit is asked for.

§ 陸 Questions we are usually asked

Eight things worth knowing before you write to us.

壹Who owns the book?

Under the default Standard Licence, the client owns personal use of the final manuscript and any commercial use named in the scope letter. Ink & East retains its baseline authorial rights, its editorial method and structural experience, and the right to display the project anonymously as a case. Under Full Rights Buyout — quoted separately, paid on top of the project fee — the client owns the work outright and the studio neither displays nor reuses any part of it.

貳How many revisions are included?

Two careful rounds, after the first manuscript is delivered. We treat each round as a real pass, not a touch-up — so two is usually enough. Work beyond two rounds is re-quoted by the hour or by stage in writing.

叄What format do I get the book in?

PDF and DOCX, always. An EPUB-ready file is available as an add-on, named in the scope letter at step 叄. Print typesetting, ISBN registration, retailer distribution, and marketing are not part of the project; if you need any of those, we can quote them as separate work, but the studio's deliverable ends at the manuscript file.

肆What if I don't like the first draft?

Step 陸 — outline confirmation — exists precisely so this does not happen. We do not begin the manuscript until the outline is signed off, because a manuscript that's been written in the wrong shape cannot be revised back into the right one cheaply. If the first draft genuinely strays from the agreed outline, the two included revision rounds are how we converge.

What we don't write into a default rule is a refund clause for taste alone. The book is private; taste varies; we'd rather have one frank conversation at the outline stage than disappoint quietly twelve weeks in.

伍Will I be credited as author, or are you ghostwriting?

Authorship and credit are named in the scope letter, not set by a default rule. Most clients are credited as author; the editor is credited inside the book, where the editor is named. For private projects where the client prefers no editor mention, the Full Rights Buyout option removes the studio's display and reuse rights together.

陸Do you accept anonymous or pseudonymous projects?

Yes. Plenty of the work is private by nature — family histories, letters to children, brand-internal manuscripts. Pseudonyms are fine; so is no public byline at all. If you'd also like the studio to keep no record of the project on its side, the Full Rights Buyout is the licence to ask about.

柒What if the project changes scope mid-way?

Out-of-scope work is re-quoted by the hour or by stage, in writing, before it begins. We'd rather pause and re-name the figure than absorb the time and quietly resent the project. If a scope change is large enough that the original quote no longer makes sense, we'll say so plainly and you can decide how to proceed.

捌How is payment structured?

By milestone, never up front in a single line. Smaller projects split 50/50 — half on signing, half before final delivery. Larger projects split 30/40/30 — thirty per cent on signing, forty per cent on the first manuscript, thirty per cent before final delivery. Which split applies, and the exact figures, are set in the scope letter at step 叄 and do not move afterwards.
