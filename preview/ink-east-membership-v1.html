— A note from the editors —

Ink & East publishes a single issue when a single issue is ready. It carries no advertising and never will. The journal stays this way because some of its readers pay a small monthly fee to keep it that way — and that fee, however quiet, is the only thing that buys the editors the patience to keep publishing slowly.

Two levels, told as two short stories rather than as a comparison table. Read them as paragraphs, not as a checklist. The level you choose is mostly about how much you want to step in — not about which features you switch on.

讀Reader· entry support

For the reader who wants the journal to keep arriving.

讀 · 入 門

To support at the Reader level is to keep the lights on for the journal — to make it possible for an issue to be quietly assembled and published when it is ready, instead of when an algorithm asks for it.

Readers see each new issue first, before it lands in the open archive a season later, and may write Reader Notes underneath the essays. Once a month they may send a Letter to the editors — the kind of slow correspondence that has been the heart of small journals since long before the internet.

The first time a Reader commissions a Custom Reading, a quiet thank-you courtesy is folded into the quote — not advertised, not the reason to join, just a small return of the favour.

贊Patron· deeper support

For the reader who wants to step in further.

贊 · 深 入

To support at the Patron level is to step in further. Everything a Reader holds, a Patron holds — and a few quiet things besides.

A Patron has a vote in what the next issue investigates (the ballot lives inside the Reading Room, and we tally it together with the editorial team), may send up to three Letters in a month, and sits at the front of the queue when they commission a Custom Reading, with a small standing courtesy on the final quote.

Once a year, when the issues of the year are gathered into a single bound volume, a printed copy is sent in the post — the only physical object the journal makes.

— Aside —

If you arrived here from the Custom Reading page, a small note: that is a separate service, kept at its own door. Membership is not how you commission a Custom Reading. Patrons happen to receive a small standing courtesy when they do, but the two products are kept apart on purpose — the Custom Reading editors take work from anyone, member or not.
